BitMatesPixel-art MMORPG DungeonSmithyD&D in the browser DungeonGameDungeon MMO in Unity

Co-founder and CTO · 2019 to 2026

A free-to-play pixel-art MMORPG. Players explore, fight bosses together, farm, craft, and trade. The characters and items are tokens on Ethereum, so what players earn belongs to them. The game brought in more than $3.2 million.

I built it full stack. I started as the only developer and wrote the first version of everything: the game, the servers, the smart contracts, the database, and the hosting. As it grew I led a team of engineers, and I stayed in the code the whole time.

What I built

  • The game server, in multithreaded Java. Chat, trading, and parties work across servers, and players can be moved between machines without being disconnected.
  • The game itself: networking, inventory, crafting, and quests.
  • The smart contracts: 10,000 characters, in-game items, a marketplace with auctions and royalties, and staking. Reworking them cut the gas cost of a transaction by about half.
  • A bridge that turns a game item into a token and back, built so nobody can duplicate an item on the way.
  • The database, the hosting, and sign-in with Discord, Google, Facebook, or Apple.

Built with: Java, Unity and C#, Solidity and Hardhat, PostgreSQL, Firestore, Docker, DigitalOcean, Google Cloud.

DungeonSmithy

2025 to now · dungeonsmithy.com

A virtual tabletop for D&D 5th edition that runs in the browser. You open a room, send your friends the link, and play. The game rolls the dice, tracks conditions, and applies the rules for you.

What I built

  • A 5e rules engine that resolves every attack, save, and spell on the server. It covers 6 classes, 257 monsters, 46 spells, and 123 magic items.
  • Grid combat with initiative, opportunity attacks, cover, line of sight, and lighting.
  • Town and street generation from a seed. I ported Watabou's city generator to TypeScript.
  • A canvas renderer that draws the world and town maps.
  • The whole stack around it: the browser client, four backend services, the database, and the deployment.

Built with: React, TypeScript, and Konva in the browser. Node.js services for the game server, room routing, sign-in, and rooms. PostgreSQL, Redis, and Kubernetes.

DungeonGame

Personal project · 2024 to now

An isometric dungeon MMO in Unity. The client and the C# server share one library, so a dungeon generated on the server is exactly the one the client draws.

  • Dungeons and an overworld generated from a seed, with rooms, monsters, props, and lights placed by code.
  • The networking for real-time play, written from scratch.
  • A Dungeon Painter tool, and command-line tools that render any room to an image.

The 3D models come from asset packs. The generation, lighting, and tools are my code.

How I build now

Over the last couple of years I've moved to building with AI coding agents, and it has changed how much I can get done. I plan the architecture, split the work into tasks, hand those to agents, review what comes back, and test the result by playing it.

The job is close to the one I already had. At BitMates I led a team of engineers. In the Air Force I led maintenance crews. In both, my part was to set the direction, check the work, and answer for what shipped. Agents write code quickly, but someone still has to know what the finished system should look like and notice when something is wrong.
